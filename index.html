Seven peoples, six places

The Davao Region has five provinces and Davao City. Its Indigenous peoples are often grouped under one word, Lumad, but they speak different languages, tell different stories and play different music.

Three of our seven belong to the Manobo family of languages: the Ata-Manobo, the Dibabawon and the Bagobo-Tagabawa. The Bagobo-Klata share the Bagobo name but have a language of their own. The Mandaya and Mansaka are close relatives, and the B'laan of the south speak a language unlike the rest. Pick a place to open its story.

We are IT students, not members of these communities. Everything here comes from published sources, retold briefly in our own words. We left out prayers, ritual words and anything a source calls sacred.

Why it matters

Stories and music are how these communities remember who they are, teach what is right, and act together.

Each tradition is an answer that exists nowhere else.

Signals are protocols

The kuratong code needs no network and still works when the power is out.

Elders are administrators

Communities already decide who may hear what. An archive must copy those rules.

Five rules for a digital archive

Consent first, under the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act.

Keep every version, with teller, place and date.

Label what is restricted, so software enforces it.

Store it with the community.

Keep the sound, not only the text.

Conclusion

We expected seven versions of the same thing. We found seven different things that sometimes touch.

A dove is an ancestor in one place and a weather sign in another. A gong paces planting, opens a prayer, or warns of danger. The overlaps show these peoples have long been neighbours. The differences show each one thought for itself.

The golden guitar was not stolen. It was thrown away when nobody listened. Technology can record a bard and protect what is sacred, but only with the community's consent and control.

Key insights

  1. Shared symbols carry different meanings.
  2. Origin stories tie identity to land.
  3. Music has jobs: prayer, courtship, work, warning.
  4. Respect for nature is taught through spirits.
  5. Preservation needs consent and an audience.
